# Traffic Accidents — Exploratory Data Analysis

## Project Overview

An exploratory data analysis project focused on identifying patterns in road traffic accidents, examining contributing factors, and understanding variations in crash severity and injury outcomes. The project applies data preprocessing, statistical analysis, and visualization techniques to uncover meaningful insights from traffic accident data.

## Objectives

- Examine dataset structure, completeness, and data quality.
- Identify the most frequent crash types and contributing factors.
- Analyze temporal patterns across hours, weekdays, and months.
- Investigate the relationship between weather, lighting, and road conditions and accident outcomes.
- Evaluate injury rates across different crash categories.
- Explore trends in reported injuries and fatalities.

## Tech Stack

- **Programming Language:** Python
- **Data Manipulation:** Pandas, NumPy
- **Data Visualization:** Matplotlib, Seaborn
- **Development Environment:** Jupyter Notebook

## Analytical Approach

- **Data Inspection and Preprocessing:** Assessed data structure, data types, missing values, and consistency.
- **Temporal Analysis:** Examined accident distributions by time of day, day of the week, and month.
- **Crash Pattern Analysis:** Investigated the distribution of crash types and contributing factors.
- **Environmental Analysis:** Explored accident patterns across weather, lighting, and road conditions.
- **Injury and Severity Analysis:** Compared injury rates and accident outcomes across relevant categories.
- **Data Visualization:** Used graphical representations to identify distributions, trends, and potential relationships.

## Key Findings

- Turning, angle, and rear-end collisions are among the most frequently recorded first-crash types.
- Accident counts show a peak during the afternoon, particularly around 5 PM.
- Friday records the highest number of accidents among the days of the week.
- October records the highest accident count among the months analyzed.
- Pedestrian crashes exhibit a high observed injury rate within the analyzed dataset.
- Rainy weather and certain darkness conditions show higher observed injury rates than their comparison categories.

*These findings describe patterns in the analyzed data and do not independently establish causation or comparative accident risk.*

## Business and Safety Relevance

The analysis demonstrates how data-driven methods can support road safety investigations by identifying recurring accident patterns, highlighting potentially vulnerable road-user categories, and examining environmental and temporal factors associated with accident outcomes. These insights can inform further investigation and evidence-based traffic safety planning.
